# Garden window texture

- Asset: `garden-seasons.png` — original generated four-season atlas, 2×2 (spring/summer/autumn/winter).
- Created with the built-in ImageGen tool (not the fallback CLI).
- This is an illustrative garden, not a live camera view of the weather location. Actual weather drives the scene's conditions.
- Final prompt:

Use case: photorealistic-natural. Asset type: a single four-season landscape texture atlas for the windows of an immersive 3D garden studio. Create a high-resolution 3072x2048 landscape image divided precisely into a borderless 2x2 grid of equal panels. All four panels show exactly the same tranquil Japanese countryside garden from exactly the same fixed eye-level camera: a gently curving stone footpath, fine layered shrubs and a graceful small deciduous tree at the left edge, a distant wooden cottage partially visible among trees, soft forested hills receding into atmospheric depth. Sky occupies the top 35 percent of each panel; keep the middle sightline open with a clear sense of distance. Top left: spring, restrained pale cherry blossoms and fresh leaves. Top right: summer, rich natural foliage. Bottom left: autumn, warm copper and russet leaves. Bottom right: winter, delicate snow on path, branches and distant rooftops. Refined landscape photography, realistic tiny leaves, natural bark and stone textures, soft neutral daylight, coherent exposure and restrained colors across all four seasons, quiet elegant composition. Important: identical terrain, horizon and camera in all four quadrants; perfectly straight division at 50 percent width and height, without any separator lines. This is an outdoor environment texture, not a window mockup. No window frames, no interior, no people, no labels, no writing, no borders, no watermarks, no collage paper edges.

The Transformer chalkboard is an original canvas drawing based on the attention equation and encoder block in [Attention Is All You Need](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762). The articulated songbird and holographic instrument are original procedural models. Existing public CC0 PBR textures are credited in `../materials/studio/CREDITS.md`.
